\noindent Dear Editor,\par\medskip
We submit our manuscript, ``Artificial Intelligence for Angiographic Sequence Analysis: A Systematic Review of Temporal Modeling, Clinical Readiness, and Research Gaps,'' for consideration as a review article in \textit{Computers in Biology and Medicine}.

\medskip
Digital subtraction angiography and fluoroscopy are read as image sequences: clinicians judge disease from how contrast fills and washes out over time. Many AI methods now analyze these sequences, but no review has examined how they use the temporal information. Following PRISMA 2020, we screened 5,064 records from PubMed/MEDLINE and Semantic Scholar, validated the LLM-assisted screening with two independent human reviewers (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.98$), and synthesized 115 studies. Most studies (82.6\%) use temporal information, but about half do so with fixed operations rather than learned models, and transformer or state-space models appear in fewer than one in ten. External validation (16.5\%), code release (21.7\%) and data release (2.6\%) are uncommon. We assess reporting against the CLAIM 2024 checklist, identify twelve research gaps, and propose a roadmap with benchmark recommendations for each.

\medskip
The review fits the journal's focus on computational methods in medicine and should interest researchers in medical image analysis as well as interventional clinicians. The review was registered retrospectively on OSF Registries (doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/6XJK7). Screening decisions, extraction records and analysis code are publicly available at https://github.com/Nafiz43/AngioVision-SLR.
